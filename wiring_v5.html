What these two boards forced to change

Neither module has the pinout a generic ESP32-S3 or C5 reference design assumes. Three things moved, and one is worth checking before you solder.

  • GPIO9 and GPIO10 are not on the S3-PRO headerThe generic map puts SCL on GPIO9 and the battery divider on GPIO10. Neither pin exists here, so SCL moved to GPIO16 and battery sense to GPIO8. GPIO8 still had to be inside GPIO1–10, because that is the whole of ADC1 — and ADC2 is unusable the moment the radio transmits.
  • The C5-MINI stops at IO15There is no GPIO23 or GPIO24 on this module, so the UART to the flight controller moved to IO4 (RX) and IO5 (TX). IO2 and IO7 were left alone because they are strapping pins, and TXD/RXD belong to the USB console.
  • Check the S3-PRO for a 5 V or VIN pin before you wire power The right-hand header shows RST and two 3V3 pins; if there is no 5 V input broken out, the board takes power through its USB-C port and you feed it from the BEC through a USB-C breakout rather than a header pin. Do not feed the BEC into a 3V3 pin — that bypasses the on-board regulator and puts 5 V on a 3.3 V rail.

Four ways to destroy hardware

  • MQ output straight to the ADC5 V into a 3.3 V pin. The pin dies, sometimes the ESP32 with it. Measure both dividers with 5 V applied before connecting them.
  • GY-87 or VL53L0X on the 5 V railTheir pull-ups tie to VCC, so 5 V there lands 5 V on SDA and SCL. Both modules go on 3V3.
  • Reversed electrolyticThe 470 µF bulk cap across the PDB vents, loudly, and coats the board. Check polarity twice.
  • Uncut stripboard under a moduleThe strip runs straight under the footprint and shorts the left pins to the right ones. Cut every one, then verify each cut with a multimeter before the LiPo goes on.
